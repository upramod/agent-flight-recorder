# Publication venue plan

Prepared: 2026-09-17

## Current state

The manuscript now has a frozen primary result, a reproducible evidence trail, a related-work position that avoids first-of-kind claims, and an explicit security-utility result. The remaining weaknesses are experimental breadth and direct comparison against strong published defenses.

## Stretch target: USENIX Security 2027, Cycle 2

Current posted dates:

- mandatory registration: January 19, 2027;
- paper submission: January 26, 2027;
- artifact submission: January 29, 2027;
- conference: August 11-13, 2027.

The venue permits up to 13 pages of body text for initial submission and requires an Open Science Appendix. The call also requires authors to review AI-generated content as if they wrote it themselves and treats fabricated references or results as academic misconduct.

Fit: strong. The work is a runtime systems-security mechanism with a public artifact and paired security evaluation.

Risk: high. Reviewers may expect direct comparison with stronger published defenses, adaptive attacks, broader model coverage, or a stronger formal security argument. The current manuscript acknowledges each gap rather than hiding it.

## Alternate stretch target: IEEE S&P 2027, second deadline

Current posted dates:

- abstract registration: November 10, 2026;
- paper submission: November 17, 2026;
- conference: May 17-20, 2027.

IEEE S&P uses anonymous review. A rejection also carries a one-year resubmission restriction for materially similar work. The shorter preparation window and that resubmission rule make this a less attractive first target for the current manuscript.

## NDSS 2027

The fall-cycle paper deadline was August 19, 2026, so the normal NDSS 2027 submission window is already closed for this project.

## Recommendation

Do not rush the paper into IEEE S&P merely because the deadline is closer. Use the current manuscript as the stable research record. Prepare it to USENIX-quality standards, including artifact documentation and anonymous-review hygiene, then make the final venue choice before the January registration deadline.

This recommendation does not reopen the benchmark cycle. No new experiment should be launched to obtain prettier results. If a direct-defense comparison or adaptive evaluation is added before submission, it must answer a declared scientific gap, use a frozen plan, and remain separate from the already-frozen v2 endpoint.
